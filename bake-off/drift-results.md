# Drift test (issue #1240 follow-up)

Run: 2026-10-12.
Script: `scripts/bake-off-drift-test.mts` (3 identical runs per slot,
fixed seed=42, prompt from `bake-off/vacant-insert.json`).

Diff is mean RGB delta in the **mask-complement region** — i.e. every pixel
*outside* the white edit rectangle. The white rect is the edit area per fal's
mask convention (white = edit, black = preserve), and OpenAI's convention
(alpha=0 = edit, alpha=255 = preserve) is converted in
`scripts/bake-off-mask-invert.mts`. Lower = more stable.

## Mask-capable candidates (with mask, mask fix applied)

| Slot | Mean delta | Max delta | Latency | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flux-pro-fill (incumbent) | ~0.006 | 8 | ~20s | $0.05/MP |
| z-image-turbo-inpaint | ~0.04-0.11 | 4 | ~8s | $0.01/MP |
| flux-kontext-lora-inpaint | ~0.039 | 2 | ~12s | $0.035/MP |

All three are essentially pixel-perfect in the unedited region. Earlier
claims that FLUX was uniquely stable were artifacts of an inverted-mask
bug; all three mask-fill candidates tie on this metric.

## Instruction-mode candidates (no mask, top visual picks)

| Slot | Mean delta | Max delta | Latency | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen-image-2-1 | **0.000** | **0** | ~66s | $0.0367/MP |
| nano-banana-2-1 | 0 to 36 (half-deterministic) | 227 | ~14s | $1 (sentinel row) |
| seedream-5-flash | 28-34 | 249 | ~19s | $0.027/image |
| seedream-5-pro | ~52 | 255 | ~130s | $0.0675/image |

**Qwen-Image-2.1 is perfectly reproducible with a fixed seed** — every
output pixel byte-identical across runs (mean delta 0.000, max 0 over
728,224 unedited pixels). Across **all** slots tested, mask-capable and
instruction-mode, this is the lowest drift number recorded.

**Nano-Banana-2.1 is half-deterministic**: two of three runs were
identical but the third differed significantly. This suggests some
internal non-determinism that fires probabilistically — not catastrophic
but unpredictable for production retry UX.

**Seedream-5-Flash drifts heavily** (~30 mean delta). It also took the
longest of any model in subjective visual review, ranking #1 in user
preference despite being the worst reproducer.

## What this means for the incumbent decision

User's subjective visual ranking (from bake-off review):
  1. seedream-5-flash (best looking, ~30 mean drift, 19s)
  2. qwen-image-2-1 (good looking, 0 mean drift, 66s)
  3. nano-banana-2-1 (good looking, 0-36 drift, 14s)
  4. flux-pro-fill (incumbent, OK looking, ~0 drift, 20s)
  5. gpt-image-2-5 (best-effort edits, 0 drift, 30s)

The drift data and the subjective ranking disagree on #1: Seedream is
both best-looking AND most-non-deterministic. Qwen is #2 in both subjective
quality and reproducibility. The right incumbent depends on whether
"best looking" or "deterministic re-runs" matters more for production.

## Architectural constraints

All three of the user's top visual picks (Seedream, Qwen, Nano-Banana)
are instruction-mode on fal — they take `image_urls: [...]` and a prompt,
with no `mask_url` field. The mask rectangle the staging editor paints
cannot be communicated to them on the wire. The current painter in
`src/components/canvas/` would not be honored by any of them.

`flux-pro-fill` and `z-image-turbo-inpaint` accept real masks. The mask
painter is meaningful for those two only.

## Recommendation paths

## Correction history

Earlier versions of this file had bake-off results that inverted the
mask convention — the masks sent to fal were black-rect-on-white, but fal
interprets white = edit, black = preserve. Every "edited" image was
actually generated by editing the *outside* of the rectangle and
preserving the rectangle. Drift numbers from that earlier run measured
the wrong region.

The fixed version of `scripts/bake-off-prep-masks.mts` produces
white-rect-on-black PNGs. `scripts/bake-off-mask-invert.mts` correctly
converts white pixels to alpha=0 for OpenAI's edits endpoint.
